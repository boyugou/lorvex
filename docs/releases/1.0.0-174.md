# Lorvex 1.0.0 (174)

**Ending a repeating task's series now reaches the other devices, in whatever
order its records arrive.** Three faults in how a generated occurrence leaves
its series are fixed, and the tasks the first one left behind are repaired.

- **Cancelled with its repeat.** Cancelling a task and ending its repeat in
  one step is what an assistant does with `cancel_series`, and what Siri's
  delete does since build 173. When the task was an occurrence generated by
  completing the one before, the device wrote a record that kept its link to
  the earlier occurrence after losing its series. Every other device refused
  that record, kept the task as it was, and logged "is not the deterministic
  successor of its declared parent" under **Settings → Diagnostics**. The
  cancel now ends the task's place in the series the way turning the repeat
  off does. A device that holds a task left in the old state repairs it the
  next time it runs and sends it again, with the earlier occurrence. The
  repair runs on the device that holds the task, so that device needs this
  build; on the reported account that is the Mac.
- **Repeat turned off, task cancelled.** Turning the repeat off on a
  generated occurrence sends two records: the task, and the earlier
  occurrence that no longer names it as the next one. A device that received
  the earlier occurrence's record first took it to mean the task was
  withdrawn, cancelled the task, and sent the cancel back. A device on this
  build leaves the task alone when the record it applies does nothing to the
  earlier occurrence except end its note of the next one, and takes the
  task's own record for what became of it. A device on an older build still
  cancels in that order, so the fix holds once every device is updated.
- **Completed again after the series ended.** Reopening the earlier
  occurrence and completing it again generates the later one again. A device
  that held the later task without its series refused the earlier
  occurrence's record with the same message and never applied the later
  one. It now applies both.

Also in this build:

- **Export after undoing a completion.** Completing a repeating task, undoing
  that, and then turning its repeat off left the generated occurrence linked
  to a task that had no series, and every later export stopped with "Task
  sync is still reconciling related records". The occurrence now drops the
  link with the repeat.
- **Share the diagnostics list.** On iPhone and iPad, **Share** at the end of
  **Settings → Diagnostics → Recent Diagnostics** sends the whole list as
  text, with the build, the system version and the device model. Until now
  the only way to get an entry off a phone was to expand it and select its
  text.
- **Siri and the app's name.** The app declares an alternative name with a
  pronunciation hint and a spoken name, for a Siri that hears "Lorvex" as
  another word. Whether it helps can only be judged by ear; the
  [roadmap](../../ROADMAP.md) lists the check.
- A change in the system calendar that arrives while the app is in the
  background no longer tries to read the store, which is closed there, and no
  longer leaves a "database access is suspended" entry. A calendar import
  that fails while a calendar view loads is logged as
  `ios.calendar.eventkit_import_failed`; `ios.settings.eventkit_import_failed`
  now means a change made in Settings.
- On iPhone Duo's outer display, the calendar's week strip no longer shows the
  next week beside the vertical bars.
- The Day and Week grids on iPhone and iPad run to the bottom of the screen
  and pass beneath the tab bar. They stopped above it before, which left an
  empty band on iPhone Duo, whose tab bar is at the side. The last hour still
  comes to rest above the bar.
- A timed task on a narrow lane of the calendar grid shows its title alone,
  in whole words, where a title beside its ring broke inside a word. A block
  too narrow for its time leaves the time out where it drew a cut one
  ("11:00 A").

## Verification

The full suites passed on the commit before the build number changed. The app
package ran 5,456 Swift Testing tests and 657 XCTest tests, 2 of them skipped.
The core package ran 2,610 XCTest tests, 24 of them skipped (23 are benchmarks
that run on request), and 1 Swift Testing test. Every static gate passed. The
records for builds 172 and 173 give "218 core tests"; that number is one of
the app package's two Swift Testing runs, and those records do not state the
core package's own count.

Thirteen new tests cover the sync change. Twelve run two in-memory devices
and hand one device's queued records to the other, in both orders wherever
the order of two records matters: cancelling a generated occurrence with its
series, alone and through its list; turning the repeat off or cancelling the
series on the later occurrence; ending the series of a reopened occurrence,
with an export on each device; completing the earlier occurrence again after
the series ended; a completion that another device overrode by marking the
task in progress; a schedule edit on another device after the repeat was
turned off; a second completion that generates nothing on a device that
never held the generated occurrence; and the repair, including a task it
cannot write, a task it cannot queue, a task stamped beyond any replay, and a
pass that cannot commit. The thirteenth checks the register intent of a
successor cancelled through a task update.

Three independent reviews read the sync change before it was committed. Each
got the diff and a description of its intent, not the author's conclusions.

- The first found the cancel itself correct and two further faults on the
  receiving device, both now fixed: a device that held the later task
  without its series refused the earlier occurrence's record once that
  occurrence was completed again, and then held the later task's record back
  for good. It also found a repair that stopped at the first task it could
  not write. Declined: repairing a task that kept its link beside a repeat
  of its own. The repair ends the earlier occurrence's record, which a device
  on an older build answers by cancelling the task, and the owner's Mac held
  no such task.
- The second found that a rule added to the receiving device after the first
  review was too broad: it kept a generated occurrence open after another
  device had marked the earlier occurrence in progress. It also found a
  cancelled successor queued with one register where the cancel now writes
  two, on a path no product surface reaches today, and a repair that rolled
  back as a whole and repeated on every pass, unlogged, when one record
  could not be queued. All three are fixed.
- The third found the narrowed rule still too broad in one case, the second
  completion that generates nothing, and proposed the condition the build
  ships: the receiving device leaves a later task alone only when the record
  it applies changes nothing about the earlier occurrence except ending its
  note of the next one. Every other case behaves as build 173 does. It also
  found three faults in the repair's failure handling, all fixed: a task
  stamped beyond any replay blocked the repair for good, a failed pass could
  go unlogged for a whole launch, and a savepoint that could not be rolled
  back was counted as one failed task.

One unrelated test failed in the last full run and was corrected. A test of
Mac Today's arrow-key order planned a task for 23:00 and assumed the order
did not depend on the clock; between 23:00 and 23:30 that task is the one
under way and leads the column. The test now reads the column at noon.

Captured headlessly on the iPhone 17 Pro simulator (iOS 26.5): the end of
Settings in light and dark, with **Share** below the diagnostics list and a
new entry reading "1s ago", and the calendar's week view in dark.

Not exercised by automation:

- Two devices over iCloud. The tests hand records between two in-memory
  stores; nothing ran against CloudKit, and no device on an older build took
  part.
- The repair on real data. The owner's Mac held two tasks in the repairable
  state when the structure of its database was read on 2026-11-22. The
  repair runs there once the Mac has this build.
- What Siri hears. The name's alternative spelling and spoken form are in the
  built metadata; whether Siri now recognizes "Lorvex" can only be judged by
  ear.

The built metadata was read in what shipped. The iOS app, its Watch app, the
Mac App Store app and the notarized Mac app each carry build 174, the spoken
name and the alternative name. The iOS package and the Mac App Store package
each hold 89 actions, every one with a declared lock policy, as in build 173.

The cases the change leaves open are listed in the
[roadmap](../../ROADMAP.md). One was found after the build was cut and is in
earlier builds too: turning the repeat off on an occurrence cancels the later
occurrences, including ones already completed, which lose their completion
time.
